Anomaly detection across more than 11 million HDFS log lines, with a Streamlit interface for review.

Data
11M+ HDFS log lines
Work
Model training and tuning
Interface
Streamlit

The problem

Finding suspicious behavior among millions of HDFS log lines is difficult to do by hand.

My contribution

I trained and tuned the anomaly detection model and helped build the Streamlit app's frontend and backend.

Approach and decisions

The team pipeline groups raw HDFS log lines by block ID and matches them to 29 event templates. It builds event-frequency vectors for Logistic Regression and Isolation Forest, and padded event sequences for LSTM and DeepLog.

Comparing frequency and sequence models makes an important tradeoff visible: event counts are insensitive to order, while sequence models can detect changes in event order. The notebook tests random and temporal splits and includes a shuffle experiment, rather than treating one headline score as a complete evaluation.

Evaluation and outcome

The saved notebook reports temporal-split F1 scores of 0.9982 for Logistic Regression, 0.7549 for Isolation Forest, 0.9973 for LSTM, and 0.3532 for DeepLog. These are recorded project results, not a new benchmark run.

The Streamlit app accepts pasted or uploaded HDFS logs, offers a complete-block sample, and lets a reviewer adjust the decision threshold. Its interface distinguishes model scores from the selected verdict.

Limitations

These results are specific to the HDFS_v1 benchmark and its block-level labels. They do not establish detection quality on a different production system. The app warns that truncated block lifecycles can change predictions; the comparatively weak DeepLog F1 also shows that a sequence model is not automatically the best detector.
