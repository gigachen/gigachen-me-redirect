Computer vision

MoneyTalks

A camera-based rupiah recognition app that speaks the detected value in Indonesian.

Coverage
7 denominations
Output
Spoken Indonesian
Interface
Flask

The problem

Reading a banknote's value visually is not equally easy for everyone.

My contribution

I trained and tuned the recognition model and helped build the Flask frontend and backend.

Approach and decisions

The team implementation combines ORB keypoints and an HSV color histogram with a Bag-of-Visual-Words representation, TF-IDF weighting, and an SVM classifier. A second path reads printed numerals with Tesseract OCR, including fuzzy denomination matching.

Decision fusion cross-checks the two signals to address confusing pairs such as 2,000 versus 20,000 rupiah. The app gates low-confidence responses instead of always announcing a denomination, and returns Indonesian speech through gTTS.

Evaluation and outcome

The app covers seven denominations from 1,000 to 100,000 rupiah. Its repository includes tests for OCR normalization, denomination extraction, feature preprocessing, and the prediction response contract.

Those tests use synthetic images and mocked models in parts of the suite. They establish implementation checks, not field accuracy. No independently verified end-to-end accuracy figure is reported here.

Accessibility and limitations

The intended users include blind and visually impaired people. Camera framing, lighting, image orientation, and the availability of speech services can affect the experience. Confidence estimates and OCR fusion do not guarantee that a spoken amount is correct.

The repository does not establish usability testing with the intended users. The project should therefore be described as an accessibility-oriented prototype, with further real-world testing needed.
