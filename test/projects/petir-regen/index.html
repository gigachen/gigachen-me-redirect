PETIR REGEN 2026

Two qualifying-round CTF challenges designed and built end to end.

The problem

The PETIR REGEN 2026 qualifying round needed original challenges in two disciplines.

My contribution

I designed and built both challenges end to end: wave-to-the-camera in digital forensics and howls-cat in AI.

Forensics: wave-to-the-camera

The published challenge distributes an MP3 and asks players to recover a two-part flag. The intended path starts with a spectrogram, then moves to file carving and EXIF inspection of an embedded image.

Using two different evidence surfaces makes the challenge more than a single hidden-string lookup. The public walkthrough names Sonic Visualiser or Audacity for the audio, Binwalk for extraction, and ExifTool for the image metadata.

AI: howls-cat

Players alter a supplied cat image so the challenge model treats it as a banana while the image stays within a Chebyshev-distance bound. The public solver converts the image to 32×32 grayscale and applies a bounded, sign-based perturbation using the provided weights.

The distance constraint requires players to work from the supplied image instead of submitting an unrelated banana picture. The resulting task connects a simple model’s decision rule with the visual effect of a small perturbation.

Outcome and limitations

Both challenges were created for the PETIR REGEN 2026 qualifying round. The repositories provide reproducible solve paths, but no verified solve counts or difficulty survey were found, so no participation or difficulty claims are made here.

The published walkthroughs reveal solution techniques. These excerpts document challenge construction; the AI task is a controlled adversarial example, not evidence of bypassing arbitrary vision models.
